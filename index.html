⚖️ Balancing Effectiveness & Imperceptibility (Slide 15)

When crafting an adversarial example x′ from an original clean input x, evasion attacks face a fundamental geometric trade-off around the classifier's decision boundary:

  • Closer to the decision boundary: The perturbation is smaller and harder for a human to spot, but the misclassification is fragile (tiny input variations or preprocessing can flip it back to the true class).
  • Farther past the decision boundary: Misclassification confidence becomes very strong (e.g., 99%), but the visual distortion becomes increasingly noticeable to a human observer.

To quantify and constrain the magnitude of the difference vector v = x′ − x across n input dimensions (pixels/channels), adversarial ML uses the general Lₚ norm:

||x′ − x||ₚ = ( |x′₁ − x₁|ᵖ + |x′₂ − x₂|ᵖ + … + |x′ₙ − xₙ|ᵖ )^(1/p) = ( ∑ᵢ₌₁ⁿ |x′ᵢ − xᵢ|ᵖ )^(1/p)

📊 Comparison of L₀, L₁, L₂, and L∞ Norms (Slides 16–18)

Norm Formula 2D Unit Ball Visual Effect on Images Common Attacks & Practicality
L∞ (Max Norm) maxᵢ |x′ᵢ − xᵢ| Square Every pixel changes by at most a tiny uniform bound ε (e.g., ε = 8/255). Very hard for humans to spot individual outliers. FGSM, L∞-PGD, HopSkipJump. Computationally trivial and widely used.
L₂ (Euclidean) √(∑ᵢ (x′ᵢ − xᵢ)²) Circle / Sphere Bounds total cumulative energy. Allows slightly larger changes on a few pixels if compensated by minimal changes elsewhere. Carlini & Wagner (C&W), L₂-PGD, HopSkipJump. Smooth, differentiable, standard benchmark.
L₁ (Manhattan) ∑ᵢ |x′ᵢ − xᵢ| Diamond Encourages sparsity along axes: concentrates large, noticeable color/brightness shifts into a moderate subset of pixels. Generally avoided for imperceptible image attacks because humans easily spot moderate clusters of strongly altered pixels.
L₀ (Pseudo-Norm) #{ i | x′ᵢ ≠ xᵢ } Coordinate Axes Counts exact number of altered pixels (e.g., 1, 3, or 5 pixels), allowing arbitrary color changes on those pixels while leaving all others untouched. One-Pixel Attack (Su et al., 2019), localized patch attacks. Non-convex & non-differentiable (hard for gradient optimization).

🤔 Why Not Other Lₚ Norms? (Slides 16 & 18)

Why not 1 < p < 2 (e.g., p = 1.5)?

While mathematically valid convex norms, values strictly between 1 and 2 offer no practical or perceptual advantage over L₂ or L∞.

Why not p > 2 (e.g., p = 3 or p = 4)?

For any p > 2, raising differences to high powers causes the sum to be heavily dominated by the single component with the largest magnitude. Geometrically, the L₄ squircle already hugs the L∞ square tightly (try toggling the L₄ overlay in the 2D Geometry tab!). Thus, the practical difference between L₄ and L∞ is negligible—yet computing fractional roots of high powers across thousands of pixels is computationally expensive, whereas L∞ simply takes maxᵢ |x′ᵢ − xᵢ|.

🧩 Spatial Localization & Limitations of Lₚ Metrics (Slide 19)

As demonstrated by Ozbulak et al. (2020), no single Lₚ norm perfectly captures human visual perception:

  • Unlocalized low-intensity noise alters nearly every pixel (huge L₀ ≈ 0.99 and moderate-high L₂), yet remains nearly invisible because L∞ is tiny.
  • Localized patches or border frames modify far fewer pixels (low L₀ ≈ 0.15–0.28 and often lower L₂), yet can be more noticeable to a human observer when concentrated into a sharp border or high-contrast patch.
